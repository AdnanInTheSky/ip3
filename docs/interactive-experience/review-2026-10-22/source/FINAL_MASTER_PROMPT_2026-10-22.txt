## Phase B — Independent scientific and methodological review

Review each analytical workbench separately.

### B1. Economics & Trade

Audit:

* Tariff, exchange-rate, elasticity, price, quantity, and receipt relationships.
* Units, percentage conventions, sign conventions, and baseline normalization.
* Correctness of synthetic scenario interpretation.
* Whether the graph conveys the mathematical model without implying empirically established responses.
* Endpoint behavior, monotonicity, and parameter sensitivity.

Ensure conclusions distinguish illustrative comparative statics from estimated real-world trade responses.

### B2. Finance & Investment

Audit:

* Complete annual cash flows, including negative Year 0 investment.
* Discounting, rate conventions, timing, and sign treatment.
* NPV/IRR arithmetic and possible non-unique or undefined IRR cases.
* Economic-versus-financial appraisal terminology.
* Public/private scenario differentiation.
* Assumptions, cost and benefit boundaries, and valuation exclusions.
* Chart, summary, data table, and exports at all years.

Do not label a simplified generic calculation as a validated ENPV/EIRR or FNPV/FIRR appraisal unless the correct framework and evidence support it.

### B3. Market & Enterprise

Audit:

* Price-volume-profit relationships.
* Elasticity-derived demand assumptions.
* Break-even mathematics.
* Fixed-cost and variable-cost treatment.
* Root finding and threshold accuracy.
* Non-positive contribution margins and other infeasible cases.
* Distinction between the selected elasticity-derived scenario and the fixed-price volume sweep.

Verify the graph does not create false certainty about future business outcomes.

### B4. Development & Impact

Audit:

* Two-period Difference-in-Differences arithmetic.
* Treatment, comparison, and constructed-counterfactual series.
* Percentage-point interpretation.
* Axis scaling for out-of-range outcomes.
* Identification assumptions, especially parallel trends.
* Appropriate disclosure of missing observations, standard errors, and confidence intervals.
* Causal language and limits of interpretation.

Do not present synthetic DiD arithmetic as an identified causal effect.

### B5. Policy & Governance

Audit:

* Staff-time, rework, process-efficiency, and opportunity-value calculations.
* Explicit assumptions and dimensional consistency.
* Interpretation of before/after components.
* Distinction between opportunity value and fiscal savings.
* Correct use of conceptual diagrams versus measured quantitative results.

Prevent hypothetical improvements from being interpreted as documented government savings or achieved institutional outcomes.

### B6. Management & Delivery

Audit:

* Capacity, throughput, package equivalents, and completion time.
* Dynamic chart horizon and extreme scenarios.
* Week-24 target and benchmark placement.
* Weekly cumulative series and export accuracy.
* Linear model assumptions and omitted dependencies.
* Distinction between throughput arithmetic and critical-path or probabilistic project forecasting.
