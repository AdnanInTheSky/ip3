Decision guide · synthetic illustration

Under which cash-flow and discount assumptions does an illustrative investment have positive NPV?

Method
An initial Year 0 investment followed by equal annual net flows. Benefits fall and operating costs rise under the selected stress assumption. NPV includes Year 0; IRR is the discount rate at which NPV is zero.
Key assumption
Year 0 investment, equal annual flows, end-of-year discounting and no residual value. Case selection changes these synthetic flows.
Reading the threshold
NPV = 0 is the arithmetic threshold at the selected discount rate. A positive NPV here is insufficient for an investment recommendation or a public economic appraisal.
Evidence needed before a real decision

Obtain dated investment, operating, replacement and residual flows; specify currency and price basis, taxes, externalities, shadow prices and financing separately.

This single teaching account is neither an independently prepared economic account nor an investor financing model. No ENPV/EIRR or FNPV/FIRR conclusion is asserted.

01 / 08
From numbers to institutions

Model the return.
Design the conditions.

Good investment advice goes beyond a spreadsheet. It connects welfare outcomes and investment returns with finance, procurement, regulations, safeguards, institutional mandates and implementation capacity.

Evidence boundary

These teaching scenarios use synthetic assumptions. They demonstrate possible analytical methods and decision outputs. They do not document client results.

Interpretation & methodology

Understand the assumptions.

An initial Year 0 investment followed by equal annual net flows. Benefits fall and operating costs rise under the selected stress assumption. NPV includes Year 0; IRR is the discount rate at which NPV is zero.

Calculation and units

Annual net flow = benefit × (1 − stress) − operating cost × (1 + stress/2). NPV = −investment + Σ(net flow/(1+r)^year). IRR solves NPV = 0 for this conventional cash-flow pattern.

In formulas, tariff, FX change, discount rate, stress, productivity, digital uptake, rework, availability and dependency drag use fractions where applicable (8% = 0.08). Slider labels show percentages. Elasticity is unitless; outcome differences use percentage points.

What this model cannot establish

Synthetic flows exclude residual value, taxes, replacement costs, inflation, financing and shadow-price adjustments. Public/private context does not by itself establish ENPV/FNPV or EIRR/FIRR. Generic NPV and IRR describe only the entered demonstration flows.

There is no empirical calibration dataset in the handoff. Changing a scenario changes its narrative; controls are a shared teaching model, except Finance, where each case also defines its own cash-flow assumptions.

Should a logistics company invest in business-continuity infrastructure?

Flood protection, resilient storage and redundant systems may reduce downtime. The investor must separate true incremental savings from overly optimistic assumptions.

Decision challenge

  • Uncertain frequency and cost of disruption
  • Insurance, repairs and downtime tracked separately
  • Unknown cost of business continuity

Illustrative decision outputs

  • Quantified avoided downtime scenarios
  • Alternative investments and financing paths
  • Stage-gated resilience investment plan

Evidence and diagnosis

  • Enterprise disruption scenarios
  • DCF · NPV / IRR
  • Insurance / residual-risk review
  • Capital budgeting

Evidence and diagnosis

  • Exposure: Price disruptions
  • Return: Stress-test savings
  • Act: Phase the investment

Commercial benefits in this hypothetical case are annual avoided losses and operating gains. Validate probabilities, timing, financing costs and residual risk before committing capital.

Can education infrastructure generate lasting social returns?

Public application · 02 / Human-capital investment

Education investments need to link learning, retention and future productivity gains to credible costs while testing whether institutions can operate and maintain new services.

Decision challenge

  • Learning gains are long-dated and uncertain
  • Capex and recurrent budgets evaluated separately
  • Weak links between costs, inclusion and benefits

Illustrative decision outputs

  • ENPV / EIRR and benefit-cost scenarios
  • Costed investment and recurrent requirements
  • Targeting, results and implementation logic

Evidence and diagnosis

  • Education economic CBA
  • Learning / earnings benefit models
  • Affordability and scenario tests
  • Institutional capacity appraisal

Evidence and diagnosis

  • Effect: Test learning pathways
  • Cost: Value full-life costs
  • Scale: Fund operations

Human-capital benefit streams depend on causal learning effects, counterfactual outcomes and earnings assumptions. The demonstration uses simplified equal annual demonstration flows—not a real education-project model.

When does a new workforce academy earn back its investment?

A firm planning an in-house skills facility must connect training expenditure to measurable productivity, retention and quality improvements rather than enrolment counts alone.

Decision challenge

  • Training and attrition costs left out
  • Demand for skills is changing

Illustrative decision outputs

Evidence and diagnosis

  • Model: Value net benefits
  • Pilot: Prove before scaling

Productivity and retention gains are hypothetical cash-equivalent benefits here; they would require direct measurement and conservative attribution in a real corporate decision.
