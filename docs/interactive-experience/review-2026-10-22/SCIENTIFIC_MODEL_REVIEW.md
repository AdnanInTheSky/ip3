## Economics & Trade

### 1. Purpose and permitted use

How sensitive are export volumes and receipts to tariffs, exchange rates and demand elasticity? Permitted use: explain mechanisms and conduct transparent synthetic sensitivity exercises. Compare each index with 100 and test the direction of change. An index below 100 is a modelled decline, not evidence of a welfare loss of the same size.

### 2. Equations and variables

t = additional tariff, d = local-currency depreciation and ε = absolute demand elasticity. t and d are fractions (12% → 0.12). Producer and buyer prices are dimensionless factors; quantity/receipts are displayed as indices with baseline 100. USD receipts are normalized value indices, not dollar amounts.

p = (1 + 0.4d)/(1 + d); b = (1+t)p; q = exp(−ε log b); USD = pq; local = pq(1+d). The equivalent p = 1−0.6d/(1+d) makes the assumed pass-through explicit.

| FX depreciation (%) | 8 | -15 | 25 | 1 |

Baseline indices are 100. FX pass-through is assumed to be 60%; elasticity is selected, not estimated. Finance defaults vary by selected case; the protocol includes explicit fixed cash-flow assumptions. Other case changes are narrative context and do not create new calibrated equations.

### 4. Independent verification and sensitivity

For permitted values, q decreases with tariff and increases with depreciation. Elasticity magnifies a decline when buyer price is above 1 and an increase when it is below 1. USD receipts need not increase with depreciation: the sign depends on ε relative to 1. At ε=1 USD receipts are invariant to d for fixed tariff. A blanket “higher elasticity reduces exports” claim would be wrong. See `scripts/explore-scientific-proof.cjs` and `build/explore-scientific.test.cjs`, plus matching model rows in the proof JSON. Tests use independently written expected identities; passing them is implementation evidence.

### 6. Limitations and misuse

The 60% coefficient is synthetic. There is no supply response, competitor response, capacity constraint or fitted trade dataset. These are scenario indices, not an official forecast. Partial-equilibrium sensitivity only; no welfare, employment or general-equilibrium estimate.

## Finance & Investment

NPV = 0 is the arithmetic threshold at the selected discount rate. A positive NPV here is insufficient for an investment recommendation or a public economic appraisal.

### 2. Equations and variables

A = B(1−s)−O(1+s/2); CF0 = −C0; CFy = A for y=1…N; NPV = −C0 + Σ A/(1+r)^y. IRR is a root of that same stream’s NPV. Independent annuity identity: NPV = A[1−(1+r)^(−N)]/r−C0; for r=0 use NA−C0.

## Market & Enterprise

### 1. Purpose and permitted use

What volume covers fixed costs at the selected price and effective variable cost? Permitted use: explain mechanisms and conduct transparent synthetic sensitivity exercises. Operating profit crosses zero at fixed cost divided by positive unit contribution. Displayed break-even rounds up to a whole unit. With contribution ≤ 0 there is no finite break-even.

### 2. Equations and variables

P = selected selling price (USD/unit), C = selected variable cost (USD/unit), u = productivity uplift fraction, ε = demand elasticity. Demand baseline is 1,200 units/month at USD120/unit. F = USD29,000/month fixed cost.

Q = 1200(P/120)^(−ε); effective cost K=C/(1+u); unit contribution c=P−K; monthly profit Π=Qc−F; revenue=QP; margin=100Π/(QP). Exact continuous zero crossing Q*=F/c if c>0. Displayed whole-unit break-even=ceil(Q*). No finite nonnegative break-even exists when c≤0 and F>0.
