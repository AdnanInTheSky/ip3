Decision guide · synthetic illustration

What volume covers fixed costs at the selected price and effective variable cost?

Method
A stylised monthly enterprise with 1,200 units of demand at a price of $120, fixed costs of $29,000, constant demand elasticity and productivity reducing variable unit cost.
Key assumption
Monthly fixed cost is $29,000; demand is 1,200 units at $120 before the selected elasticity adjustment. Productivity reduces variable cost only.
Reading the threshold
Operating profit crosses zero at fixed cost divided by positive unit contribution. Displayed break-even rounds up to a whole unit. With contribution ≤ 0 there is no finite break-even.
Evidence needed before a real decision

Validate transaction-level demand, capacity, variable and fixed costs, seasonality and competitor responses; investigate whether demand at the proposed price is attainable.

The volume sweep holds price and effective unit cost fixed; it is not a second demand curve or a forecast of sales.

All cases describe hypothetical applications. No completed engagement, client endorsement or empirical outcome is asserted. The unit-economics model is a teaching scenario; it does not use real company information or make investment recommendations.

Interpretation & methodology

Understand the assumptions.

A stylised monthly enterprise with 1,200 units of demand at a price of $120, fixed costs of $29,000, constant demand elasticity and productivity reducing variable unit cost.

Calculation and units

Units = 1200 × (price/120)^(−elasticity); effective cost = variable cost/(1+productivity); profit = units × (price−effective cost) − 29000; break-even units = ceil(29000/contribution) when contribution > 0.

In formulas, tariff, FX change, discount rate, stress, productivity, digital uptake, rework, availability and dependency drag use fractions where applicable (8% = 0.08). Slider labels show percentages. Elasticity is unitless; outcome differences use percentage points.

What this model cannot establish

Demand and cost assumptions are synthetic. No taxes, credit, competitors or capacity constraints are modelled. Nonpositive unit contribution means break-even is unattainable under these assumptions.

There is no empirical calibration dataset in the handoff. Changing a scenario changes its narrative; controls are a shared teaching model, except Finance, where each case also defines its own cash-flow assumptions.
