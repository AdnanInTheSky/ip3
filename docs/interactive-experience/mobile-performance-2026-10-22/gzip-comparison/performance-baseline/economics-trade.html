Decision guide · synthetic illustration

How sensitive are export volumes and receipts to tariffs, exchange rates and demand elasticity?

Method
Constant-elasticity demand with an assumed 60% exchange-rate pass-through. All indices use a baseline of 100. Positive FX change means depreciation: more local currency per US dollar.
Key assumption
Baseline indices are 100. FX pass-through is assumed to be 60%; elasticity is selected, not estimated.
Reading the threshold
Compare each index with 100 and test the direction of change. An index below 100 is a modelled decline, not evidence of a welfare loss of the same size.
Evidence needed before a real decision

Obtain product–destination tariffs, transaction prices, volumes, capacity and competitor data; estimate elasticities and pass-through before advising on policy.

Partial-equilibrium sensitivity only; no welfare, employment or general-equilibrium estimate.

From economic signals to market outcomes

Interpret the incentives.
Change the conditions.

Strong economic advice is more than a chart or a recommendation. It joins credible research, public institutions, enterprise realities and implementation discipline — with results that can be monitored and challenged.

Explicit about uncertainty.

The scenarios demonstrate analytical methods and possible decision outputs. Assumptions are synthetic. These hypothetical examples do not document client results.

Interpretation & methodology

Understand the assumptions.

Constant-elasticity demand with an assumed 60% exchange-rate pass-through. All indices use a baseline of 100. Positive FX change means depreciation: more local currency per US dollar.

Calculation and units

Producer price = 1 − 0.6d/(1+d); buyer price = (1+t) × producer price; quantity = buyer price^(−ε); USD receipts = producer price × quantity; local receipts = USD receipts × (1+d).

In formulas, tariff, FX change, discount rate, stress, productivity, digital uptake, rework, availability and dependency drag use fractions where applicable (8% = 0.08). Slider labels show percentages. Elasticity is unitless; outcome differences use percentage points.

What this model cannot establish

The 60% coefficient is synthetic. There is no supply response, competitor response, capacity constraint or fitted trade dataset. These are scenario indices, not an official forecast.

There is no empirical calibration dataset in the handoff. Changing a scenario changes its narrative; controls are a shared teaching model, except Finance, where each case also defines its own cash-flow assumptions.

What does the loss of a tariff preference mean for an exporter?

Private application · 01 / EXPORTER MARKET-ACCESS STRATEGY

When preferential access changes, the immediate impact depends on buyers, margins, country of origin, product mix and substitution possibilities—not a headline tariff alone.

Decision challenge

  • Price competitiveness tied to preferences
  • Country and buyer concentration poorly mapped
  • Limited analysis of switching and certification costs

Illustrative decision outputs

  • Buyer- and product-level tariff exposure map
  • Market diversification and pricing scenarios
  • Investment, compliance and transition priorities

Evidence and diagnosis

  • HS-product / destination mapping
  • Tariff and rules-of-origin screening
  • Unit cost and margin analysis
  • Buyer demand scenarios

Evidence and diagnosis

  • Map: Identify exposure
  • Adapt: Reprice or diversify
  • Stage: Reinvest selectively

Turning tariff uncertainty into a policy response.

Public application · 02 / TRADE POLICY AND TARIFF SHOCKS

A tariff announcement is not an economy-wide outcome. Its transmission depends on sector composition, demand elasticity, exchange rates, value chains and partner responses.

Decision challenge

  • Tariff headlines mistaken for predicted export losses
  • Product exposure and elasticities not reconciled
  • Response options lack distributional review

Illustrative decision outputs

  • Sector-by-market exposure and uncertainty bands
  • Negotiation, adjustment and support options
  • Decision triggers and monitoring indicators

Evidence and diagnosis

  • Effective tariff / price wedges
  • Partial-equilibrium sensitivity
  • Elasticity ranges and FX scenarios
  • Stakeholder and welfare analysis

Evidence and diagnosis

  • Scope: Find affected sectors
  • Test: Run scenarios
  • Respond: Protect competitiveness

Protecting margins when foreign market conditions turn.

Private application · 02 / EXPORT PRICE AND DEMAND RISK

An exporting firm may face higher buyer prices, weaker orders and fluctuating exchange rates at the same time. A useful strategy separates price, volume, margin and local-currency cash flow.

Decision challenge

  • FX revenue confused with real profitability
  • Buyer price sensitivity untested
  • Order concentration and input exposure hidden

Illustrative decision outputs

  • Scenario-tested price and volume assumptions
  • Buyer segmentation and market alternatives
  • Hedging, sourcing and cost-reduction decisions

Evidence and diagnosis

  • Cost and margin waterfalls
  • Export-demand scenarios
  • FX pass-through sensitivity
  • Competitive positioning

Evidence and diagnosis

  • Quantify: Separate price and volume
  • Hedge: Manage cash-flow risk
  • Pivot: Target resilient buyers

Making invisible trade barriers visible.

Public application · 03 / TRADE IN SERVICES

Services exports may face licensing, payment, data-flow, skills and recognition barriers that do not appear as conventional border tariffs. Good diagnostics identify which constraints can actually change.

Decision challenge

  • Fragmented information on cross-border services
  • Regulatory and payments constraints unmapped
  • Weak classification and firm-level evidence

Illustrative decision outputs

  • Tradable service and market opportunity map
  • Ranked regulatory and transaction-cost reforms
  • Measurable policy indicators and dialogue plan

Evidence and diagnosis

  • Services trade classification
  • Firm and provider interviews
  • Regulatory restriction mapping
  • Trade cost and market-demand analysis

Evidence and diagnosis

  • Classify: Understand transactions
  • Reform: Remove binding frictions
  • Measure: Track exportability

Choosing where a digital exporter should compete.

Decision challenge

Illustrative decision outputs

  • Prioritised target buyers and jurisdictions
  • Unit economics and compliance checklist
  • Pilot-to-scale milestones and learning metrics

Evidence and diagnosis

  • Serviceable market sizing
  • Buyer interviews and market tests
  • CAC / margin economics
  • Regulatory and FX friction review

Evidence and diagnosis

  • Select: Find reachable niches
  • Validate: Test paid demand
  • Scale: Invest after evidence

Testing whether technology adoption actually pays.

Illustrative decision outputs

Evidence and diagnosis

  • Energy and productivity measurements

  • Cost-effectiveness scenarios
