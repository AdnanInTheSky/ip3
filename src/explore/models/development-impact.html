From activity to additionality.

Outputs tell us what was delivered. Outcomes tell us what changed. Credible evaluation investigates what change can be attributed to the intervention.

The IP3 evidence journey

Five questions.
One accountability chain.

Development results are not a graphic of rising numbers. They are a defensible sequence from need, to design, to implementation, to credible evidence, to decisions that improve real lives.

01 / DIAGNOSE

What problem?

Baseline conditions, lived experiences, institutional gaps and binding constraints.

02 / DESIGN

What pathway?

Theory of change, targeting, incentives and implementation feasibility.

03 / MEASURE

What changed?

Reliable indicators, surveys, monitoring systems and outcome trajectories.

04 / ATTRIBUTE

Why did it change?

Comparison groups, causal designs, triangulation and uncertainty.

05 / ADAPT

What next?

Decision-grade learning, value for money, scale-up and institutional ownership.

      Research integrity: DiD = (treated endline − treated baseline) − (comparison endline − comparison baseline). Results are percentage-point differences. The dashed line is a trend-adjusted counterfactual, which may fall outside the 0–100% range under extreme inputs. A causal interpretation requires credible parallel-trends and other identification assumptions; a two-period visual alone cannot establish them. Values are synthetic, not outcomes from IP3 projects.

      Good evaluation does more than report success. It tells governments, donors and businesses when to invest, what to redesign, who benefits, who is excluded and which outcomes are worth scaling.
